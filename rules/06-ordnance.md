# 06 — Ordnance (pp. 72–87)

Ordnance = torpedoes and attack craft (fighters, bombers, assault boats, torpedo bombers). All ordnance attacks are represented by **markers** moved across the table during each Ordnance Phase. If an Ordnance marker contacts a ship or another Ordnance marker, it makes an attack.

## Launching Ordnance (p. 73)

- Ships with torpedoes and/or launch bays can use ordnance.
- Ordnance is **declared, placed on the table and launched at the end of the Shooting Phase** (put the marker on the ship's base to show it fired), but it **moves and attacks during the Ordnance Phase**.
- Launching **any amount** expends that ordnance type for the turn; the ship must **Reload Ordnance** before launching that type again. (A carrier that can only launch 2 of its 4 bays due to limits can't "save" the other 2.) Same for torpedoes (no launch limits for torpedoes).
- Torpedoes and attack craft are tracked separately: launching only one type does not expend the other. *E.g. an Imperial Dictator launches attack craft but not torpedoes; next turn it fails to Reload — it may still launch its torpedoes.*
- A ship that has never launched, or has successfully reloaded, stays loaded for as many turns as it doesn't launch, regardless of later special orders. Crippled and/or braced still affect launchers and bays normally (halved).

### Fleet Ordnance Limits (p. 73)
- A fleet may not have more **attack craft markers in play** than its total number of available **launch bays** (fleet-wide, accounting for crippled/lost ships). Fleets that "can't run out of ordnance" (Ork Space Hulks, Tyranids…) count double.
- Any carrier that has reloaded may launch (even partially) as long as the fleet total isn't exceeded.
- If more attack craft are in play than available bays, the player may not launch any that turn — but may **recall** markers in play (remove immediately, not used to attack) to launch new ones from a reloaded ship.
- This rule does not apply to torpedoes.

## Reload Ordnance (p. 74)
- Ships start the game with torpedoes loaded and attack craft ready.
- After launching, a ship must pass a **Reload Ordnance** special order before launching again. Once reloaded, it may launch that Shooting Phase or keep it ready for later. Track loaded status on the roster.

## Moving Ordnance (p. 74)
- In the Ordnance Phase, players move and attack with all their ordnance, including that launched in previous turns.
- If both players have ordnance to move, **the player whose turn it is moves all theirs first**.
- Each ordnance has a **speed** = distance moved each Ordnance Phase.
- Markers in a wave or salvo must be spread **in contact with each other** and cannot be stacked.

### Ordnance and Blast Markers (p. 75)
- If an Ordnance marker passes through Blast Markers during its move, roll a D6: **removed on a 6**.
- Ordnance attacking a ship with BMs in base contact must also take this test.
- Only **one roll** per move per marker/wave/salvo, regardless of how many BMs.

## Shooting at Ordnance (p. 75)
- Ordnance can be shot at in the **Shooting Phase** with direct fire weapons.
- No Ld check needed to ignore closer *ships* in order to shoot ordnance, nor to ignore ordnance that is the closest target. A Ld check is needed to split fire between ordnance targets, and to fire at an ordnance marker/wave/salvo that is not the closest enemy ordnance in range.
- Weapons batteries use the **Ordnance column** (column E) of the Gunnery Table; aspect is ignored, but range and BM column shifts apply.
- **Lances and weapons batteries both need 6s to hit ordnance.**
- A hit **removes** the Ordnance marker. Ordnance caught in a nova cannon blast is removed.

## Ordnance Attacks (p. 75)
- Resolved **immediately** on contact — including in the Movement Phase when a ship moves into enemy ordnance.
- **All ordnance attacks ignore the target's shields.**
- Ordnance must attack the **first** ordnance or vessel it contacts (when applicable), not necessarily the closest. (A fighter can't ignore a small salvo it touches to hit a bigger one; small escort salvoes can clear fighters for bigger salvoes.)
- If two same-size-base ships effectively occupy the same 2D position, the attacking player chooses which is attacked.

## Turrets (p. 80)

Turrets fire immediately when ordnance touches the ship's base.

- **Vs torpedo salvoes**: roll a D6 per turret; each **4+** reduces the salvo's Strength by 1.
- **Vs attack craft**: roll a D6 per turret; each **4+** destroys one squadron (marker).
- Turrets fire against **every** torpedo salvo attacking the ship in an Ordnance Phase, **or** against every attack craft wave — **not both** in the same phase.

### Massing turrets
- Ships in base contact may mass turrets: each adds **+1** turret die to the ship under attack, max **+3** (no ship can mass with more than three others).
- Massing ships take on the same restriction (torpedoes or attack craft, not both, that phase).
- Only the attacked ship's own turret value reduces bomber attacks.
- Braced ships can mass and be massed for. Crippled ships cannot mass turrets but can be helped. A ship with 0 turrets (Eldar, hulks) can't contribute but can benefit.
- Ships only benefit from massed turrets before/after the Movement Phase, not during (one exception: a ship that touches ordnance at the same moment a friendly ship moves into contact with it).

## Types of Ordnance

### Torpedoes (pp. 76–78)
- Launched from torpedo tubes (normally prow). A salvo has a **Strength** (number of torpedoes) and **Speed**.
- Represented by a Strength-3-sized marker (no more than **2.5 cm wide**) with D6(s) showing the actual strength.
- **On launch**: place the marker **at the end of its full move** for the launch turn, completely within the correct fire arc; then retrace its path, resolving interactions/reducing strength as appropriate. The marker may technically be in multiple arcs during placement, but may not interact with anything outside the torpedoes' actual fire arc, however close.
- Standard torpedoes move in a **straight line**, their **full speed** each Ordnance Phase (cannot vary speed), until they detonate or leave the table. When moving, the centre of the salvo stays on the same line of fire.
- **Attack**: if the marker contacts a ship's base (**friend or foe**), roll **1D6 per point of Strength**; each die **≥ the ship's Armour** = 1 damage. **Shields are ignored.** (Turrets fire first, reducing Strength.)
- The salvo continues moving after the attack, **Strength reduced by 1 for every hit it inflicted**. It immediately attacks any other ship it contacts.
- Torpedoes with an automatic re-roll to hit must use it, even if the target is already destroyed.
- No torpedo marker can attack the same target **more than once per full turn**.
- Torpedoes don't normally ignore targets in front of them, **including friendly ships**! A ship in base contact with a friendly vessel may "shoot through" that friend's base. Ships not in a squadron can't combine salvoes.
- Torpedoes don't normally ignore hulks in their path (boarding torpedoes may; guided torpedoes may steer away).

*Example:* a Strength 6 salvo hits a ship: 6D6 rolled, 3 hits. Salvo is reduced to Strength 3 and continues up to its full 30 cm move.

**Premature detonation** — the whole salvo is removed if:
- it moves through any Blast Markers and rolls a **6** on a D6;
- it is fired on by direct fire and **any hits** are scored;
- it comes into contact with another **Torpedo** or **Fighter** marker.

**Splitting salvoes**
- A single ship launching **6 or fewer** torpedoes cannot split its salvo.
- A single ship launching **more than 6** may split into two markers going in **different directions** (not at the same target/direction), each at least 3 torpedoes.
- Squadrons need not fire their torpedoes in a single salvo.

**Turning torpedoes** (only types allowed to): turn from the centre of the marker at the start of the Ordnance Phase; never in the phase they were launched; no edge may move more than max speed.

### Boarding Torpedoes (p. 78)
- Launched like ordinary torpedoes (only by ships listed as able). Can't launch ordinary and boarding torpedoes in the same turn.
- May make a single **45° turn** at the start of every Ordnance Phase (not on launch turn). May ignore hulks.
- On contact they attack like torpedoes (turrets, to-hit), but **each hit makes a Hit-and-Run attack** instead of 1 damage.
- Removed by contact with any other torpedoes except friendly boarding torpedoes. Don't attack friendly ships (incl. hulks), and aren't removed by friendly fighters.

### Attack Craft (p. 79)
- Launched from **launch bays**, each bay launches one **squadron** (one 20 mm square marker). E.g. a Dictator with 4 bays launches 4 squadrons. The player chooses the mix (fighters, bombers, assault boats…) at launch.
- Attack craft **turn freely and move in any direction** up to their speed.
- Attack craft contacting enemy ordnance or ships (or fighters contacting any torpedo marker) must attack. They can avoid/ignore targets/obstructions they aren't actually in contact with, but can't "fly through" enemy ship bases. They may target a small-base ship "hiding" inside a large base's footprint if they can reach it.
- An assault boat or bomber **wave that destroys a ship is entirely expended**, even if some markers hadn't attacked yet.

### Fighters (pp. 81–83)
- **Vs ordnance markers**: both the fighter and the enemy marker are removed. Fighters must always interact with ordnance they contact. Fighters ignore friendly ordnance but destroy any torpedoes (unless noted, e.g. friendly boarding torpedoes).
- **Vs ships**: no effect; the fighter marker stays in play.
- **Combat Air Patrol (CAP)**: fighters in base contact with a friendly ship may go on CAP — they move with the ship in the Movement Phase (staying in contact) but then **may not move in the Ordnance Phase**. They screen the ship against enemy ordnance (even mid-move). Multiple CAP fighters are independent markers, not a wave. Fighters can leave CAP only at the start of the owner's Movement Phase, or during the owner's part of the opponent's Ordnance Phase. Only fighters (or craft that behave as fighters) can be CAP. CAP fighters don't attack torpedoes launched by the ship they protect or friendly ships in base contact.
- **Turret suppression** (p. 83): each fighter in a bomber wave attacking a ship adds **+1 attack** to the wave's total, whether or not shot down by turrets, up to the number of **surviving bombers**. Needs at least one surviving bomber. Turrets remove fighters first. Fighters intercepted before reaching the target add nothing.

### Bombers (p. 83)
- **Vs enemy fighters**: both removed.
- **Vs other ordnance**: no effect; both stay.
- **Vs enemy ships**: each surviving bomber squadron makes **D6 attacks, minus 1 per turret on the target** (minimum 0). Each attack is a D6 roll to hit against the ship's **lowest Armour value**. Remove the bombers after attacking.
- Turrets **always** reduce bomber attacks, even if they were used against torpedoes that phase. Massed turrets don't reduce bomber attacks.
- *Example:* 2 bomber squadrons attack a Murder (2 turrets). It rolls 2 turret dice; survivors make D6−2 attacks each.

### Torpedo Bombers (p. 84)
- Launched like other attack craft; speed **20 cm**; count as bombers for interception.
- At the start of any Ordnance Phase (not the one they were launched in) a squadron may be replaced by a **Strength 2 torpedo salvo**, which acts as normal torpedoes but is removed at the end of that Ordnance Phase. A wave may combine salvoes.

### Assault Boats (p. 85)
- **Vs enemy fighters**: both removed.
- **Vs other ordnance**: no effect.
- **Vs enemy ships**: after turrets, each surviving squadron makes a **Hit-and-Run** attack (see [07-end-phase.md](07-end-phase.md#hit-and-run-attacks-pp-9192)). Then removed.
- Assault boats move **30 cm** in any direction.

## Waves of Attack Craft (p. 85)
- Launched squadrons may go individually or be combined into **waves** at launch (only at launch; once split, can't recombine). Interactions are always resolved marker to marker.
- A wave's markers touch (not stacked), arranged in the smallest circumference possible (block of 4, 2×3, etc.), and move at the speed of the slowest.
- Enemy fighters/turrets must remove **fighters first** in a wave.
- **Turrets fire only once at the whole wave.**
- Downsides: if a wave is hit by direct fire (gunnery, lances, nova cannon) **the whole wave is destroyed**; if it rolls a 6 moving through BMs, **the whole wave is removed**; the whole wave is removed after attacking a ship.

## Resilient Attack Craft (pp. 86–87)
- Some attack craft (Thunderhawks, Eldar fighters…) have a **4+ save against other ordnance**, usable once per Ordnance Phase. If saved, they stop where the interaction happened and lose the save for the rest of that phase. Failed → removed. A second interaction in the same phase removes them automatically. The attacking player decides the order of interactions.
- Two resilient markers that both save stay in contact until the next Ordnance Phase.
- In a wave, saved markers stop and split off; the rest continue.
