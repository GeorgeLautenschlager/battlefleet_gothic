# 10 — The Battlefield & Celestial Phenomena (pp. 102–116)

## Table size (p. 102)
- Typical battlefield: **6'–8' long (≈180–240 cm)** by **4'–6' wide (≈120–180 cm)**. Smaller areas work with proportionately smaller forces.

## Placing Celestial Phenomena (pp. 104–105)
Any fair method works; major features (e.g. a table-spanning asteroid belt) should be agreed with the opponent.

- **Method 1**: one player places all phenomena; the opponent picks which table edge to deploy from.
- **Method 2**: divide the table into **60 cm squares**. Roll a D6 per square: on **4+** it contains phenomena. Roll a D6 (or agree) to pick a **battlezone generator** for the whole battlefield, then roll on it for each such square. Place anywhere in the square, not on top of each other.
- **Method 3**: each player secretly chooses a battlezone number (1–6) and adds it to their fleet's attack rating; highest total picks the battlezone.

## Battlezone Generators (pp. 107–108)

Roll a D6 for each area containing phenomena.

| D6 | 1. Flare Region | 2. Mercurial Zone | 3. Inner Biosphere | 4. Primary Biosphere | 5. Outer Reaches | 6. Deep Space |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solar flare | Solar flare | Roll again: 1–3 radiation burst, 4–6 solar flare | Asteroid field | D3+1 asteroid fields | D3 asteroid fields |
| 2 | Solar flare | Radiation burst | Asteroid field | D3 asteroid fields | D3 asteroid fields | Asteroid field |
| 3 | Radiation burst | Asteroid field | D3 asteroid fields | Gas/dust cloud | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds |
| 4 | Asteroid field | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds | Gas/dust cloud | Gas/dust cloud |
| 5 | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds | Planet (1–5 small, 6 medium)* | Planet (1–3 small, 4–6 large)* | Warp rift |
| 6 | Planet (1–5 small, 6 medium)* | Planet (1–5 small, 6 medium)* | Planet (1–5 small, 6 medium)* | Planet (1–5 small, 6 medium)* | Planet (1–3 small, 4–6 large)* | Small planet (rogue)* |

\* Maximum **one planet** on the table; if a second is generated, roll again. Roll for moons. A **large** planet has rings on a D6 roll of 4+ (note: the Ringed Planets section says 5–6).

## Tabletop Features (p. 109)

- Features are placed relative to the nearest star. Determine the **sunward** table edge with a D6: **1** = left edge, **2–3** = far/top edge, **4–5** = near/bottom edge, **6** = right edge (per the rulebook diagram).
- **Any celestial phenomenon affects a ship as soon as it contacts the ship's base.** It only blocks fire if it physically blocks line of sight from the firer's stem to the target's stem.
- Warp drive implosions ignore celestial phenomena for line of sight (being inside/behind an asteroid field doesn't matter for the 3D6 cm radius).

### Gas and Dust Clouds (pp. 109–110)
- **Effects**: same as a **single Blast Marker** in all respects (firing, movement, shields, Leadership, ordnance). Eldar and kin may take a Ld check to ignore all effects (escorts re-roll for free).
- A 0-shield ship that explodes due to cloud contact: the explosion originates where it entered the cloud.
- **Placement**: bands parallel to the sunward edge, **D6×2 cm wide**, **D6×5 cm long**.

### Asteroid Fields (pp. 110–111)
- **Blocks line of fire**; torpedoes that strike it are **detonated**.
- Ships moving through, or contacting the edge of, an asteroid field must pass a **Leadership test on 2D6** (**3D6 if on All Ahead Full**). Escorts may re-roll a failure (second roll stands).
- **Failed**: **D6 damage** from asteroid impacts; **shields block damage normally** (but no Blast Markers are placed for shield hits — though the ship is still slowed 5 cm as if it moved through BMs that turn).
- Squadrons test once for the whole squadron. Capital ship squadron fails → each ship that contacts the field takes D6. Escort squadrons re-roll for free; if still failed, D6 damage is distributed among escorts that contacted the field, in the order they moved. Shields (not holofields) work.
- **Hulks** drifting into an asteroid field are destroyed.
- **Attack craft** moving through an asteroid field are destroyed on a D6 roll of **6**.
- **Shooting**: ships cannot shoot into or out of an asteroid field. Ships both inside a field may shoot each other: lances and batteries max **10 cm** range, **no nova cannon**, **no torpedoes**; no left column shift for being within 10 cm. Attack craft work but roll a D6 each Ordnance Phase they remain inside (removed on a 6, per wave/marker). Braced or crippled ships can't shoot inside a field.
- **Shooting at an asteroid field**: requires a Ld test even if it's the only target; treat as an Ordnance target. Each 6 places a BM on the field facing the shooter. Each End Phase each field loses D6 such BMs (separately from normal BM removal).
- **Placement**: parallel to sunward edge, **D3×5 cm wide** by **D3×5 cm long**.

### Warp Rifts (pp. 111–112)
- **Blocks line of fire**; torpedoes that strike it detonate. Hulks drifting in disappear (can't be salvaged). Attack craft moving in are destroyed.
- Ships moving into a rift must pass a **Ld test on 3D6**. **Pass**: may be repositioned up to **2D6×10 cm** away from the rift, facing any direction. **Fail**: the ship disappears from the battle — lost in the warp. After the game roll a D6 per lost ship: **1** = lost forever; **2–6** = eventually returns.
- **Placement**: **D3×5 cm wide**, **D3×10 cm long**.

### Planets (pp. 112–113)
- Represented by a template or model.
- A ship whose **stem** is on a planet template (not merely base contact/overlap) can see out and be seen; ships on the same template can all see each other. Otherwise planets block line of sight.
- **Torpedoes are destroyed** when they contact the template's edge (either direction).
- Hulks drifting into a planet are destroyed. Ships may move "through" a planet (over/under).
- **Sizes**: small up to **15 cm** diameter; medium **16–25 cm**; large **26–50 cm**.
- **Gravity wells** (from template edge): small up to **10 cm**; medium up to **15 cm**; large up to **30 cm**.
- **Gravity well turns**: ships in a gravity well may make a **free 45° turn at the beginning and/or end of their move**, always **towards the planet** (toward the centre of the well, or ≤ 45°, whichever is less). No minimum distance needed. Usable even when the ship can't normally turn (All Ahead Full, Lock On), and combinable with Come To New Heading. The ship must actually be in the well at that moment.
- **Orbits**: a ship in a gravity well may enter **high orbit** (needn't move, but if stationary it is targeted on the **Defences** column) or **low orbit** (removed from play; placed on a separate low-orbit table if the scenario requires). Ships leaving low orbit are placed touching the outer edge of the planet template.
- **Placement**: usually only one planet on the table.

### Ringed Planets (p. 113)
- A **large** planet has rings on a D6 roll of **5–6**. Place **D3 rings**; each ring roll D6: 1–4 gas/dust, 5–6 asteroid. Each ring is **D6 cm wide** and begins **D6×5 cm** from the planet's edge.

### Moons (p. 113)
- Moons count as **small planets** in all respects (including gravity wells).
- Medium planets have **D3−1** moons, large planets **D6−2**. Moons up to **5 cm** diameter, placed **2D6×10 cm** from the planet in a random direction.

## Tabletop Effects (pp. 114–115)

These affect the whole battlefield.

### Fighting Sunward (p. 114)
- Only in the **Flare Region, Mercurial Zone and Inner Biosphere**.
- Firing towards the sunward edge **doubles the range column shifts**: long range (> 30 cm) = **two shifts right**; short range (≤ 15 cm) = **two shifts left**.
- To check: place the bearing compass over the firer with the arrows parallel to the sunward edge; if the target is in the arc facing the sunward edge, you're shooting sunward.

### Solar Flares (p. 115)
- Roll a D6 at the start of each turn (one per flare generated). On a **6** a flare occurs — **only once per game**; stop rolling after.
- Each ship gets **one Blast Marker placed sunward** of it. Ships **without shields** take **1 hit**, and a critical on 4+. Each Ordnance marker is removed on 4+.
- Eldar and kin may test Ld to ignore (escorts re-roll free); if passed, they turn directly away and move 2D6 cm.

### Radiation Bursts (p. 115)
- Roll a D6 at the start of each turn (one per burst generated). On **5–6** a burst occurs (max one per turn).
- Roll a D6 for the **interference level**: all ships reduce **Leadership** by that amount for the rest of the turn.
- Fleet Commanders may only use re-rolls for Command checks for their own ship/squadron during radiation bursts.

## Fighting in Low Orbit (p. 116)
- Use a separate low-orbit table (≈45–60 cm × 90–135 cm); one long edge is the planet. Ships in a gravity well may enter low orbit **at the start of any of their turns** — place touching the edge farthest from the planet.
- Ships in low orbit needn't move, and capital ships needn't move a minimum distance before turning.
- All firepower suffers **one column shift right**; **lances and nova cannon need a 4+ roll to fire**; **ships cannot fire torpedoes**.
- At the start of each player's turn, ships within **45 cm** of the planet edge are pulled directly toward it (no facing change): small planet **5 cm**, medium **8 cm**, large **10 cm**. Ships pulled off the planet edge are **destroyed**.
- Escorts and transports voluntarily moving off the planet edge have **landed** (removed); capital ships can't land.
- Moving off any other edge = leaving low orbit; place on the main table touching the planet. A ship may not enter and leave low orbit in the same turn.
- Option: represent low orbit on paper at 1 mm : 1 cm scale.
