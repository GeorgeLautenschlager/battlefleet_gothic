# 03 — The Movement Phase (pp. 52–57)

## Basic Moves (p. 53)

1. Choose a ship to move.
2. Move it up to its maximum move distance.
3. Choose another ship.

- A player **must** move their ships unless they use **Burn Retros** to remain stationary.
- A ship must move **at least 5 cm** to **not** count as **Defences** on the Gunnery Table (i.e. a ship that moves less than 5 cm / stands still is targeted on the Defences column).
- Ships are always moved **one at a time**, never simultaneously (even in a squadron), in any order the owner wishes (p. 57).

## Move Distance (p. 53)

- All ships can move up to their **Speed** (e.g. Lunar cruiser: 20 cm).
- Speed is increased by **All Ahead Full**, and can be reduced by damage (crippling, Thrusters Damaged) and by moving through **Blast Markers** (−5 cm).

### Minimum Move Distance
- Ships must always move **at least half their speed** unless on **Burn Retros**.
- Ships unable to move half their speed (due to damage, Blast Markers, etc.) must move the maximum possible distance instead.

### Special orders affecting movement (p. 54)
- **All Ahead Full**: move faster (+4D6 cm), must move full distance, **cannot turn**, weapons less effective. Good for closing on a distant foe or escaping.
- **Burn Retros**: move slower than half speed or stand still; can make a single turn **without moving forward first**; weapons less effective.
- **Come To New Heading**: make an **extra turn**; weapons less effective.
- Full effects table: [01-fundamentals.md](01-fundamentals.md#special-orders-pp-4647).

## Turning (p. 54)

- Ships move in a **straight line directly ahead**, but may make **one turn** during their move (unless a special order prevents it).
- Max turn angle is **45° or 90°** as per the ship's Turns characteristic (Lunar: 45°).
- Only forward movement made **in the current Movement Phase** counts toward the minimum distance before turning.
- Distance a ship must move **before** it may turn:
  - **Battleship**: 15 cm
  - **Cruiser**: 10 cm
  - **Escort**: may turn at any point in its move.
- Come To New Heading's second turn follows the same restriction (cruiser moves 10 cm, turns, moves at least 10 cm more, turns again).

## All Ahead Full — Ramming Speed! (pp. 55–56)

- Only a ship on **All Ahead Full** may attempt to ram. A ship may attempt to ram **at most one** ship per Movement Phase.
- When the All Ahead Full command check is passed, the owner **declares the ram target and makes the ramming Leadership test** before rolling the extra 4D6 cm. The choice cannot be changed, even if the rammer ends up touching several vessels or fails to reach the target.
- No separate leadership check is needed to ignore closer targets in favour of a farther ram target.
- The ship **must move its FULL distance**, including the All Ahead Full extra.
- **Ram success is by base contact**: if any part of the rammer's base touches any part of the target's base during the move, pause and resolve the ram.
- **Leadership test to ram** — size order (biggest → smallest): **Defence > Battleship > Cruiser > Escort**:
  - Target **smaller** type than rammer: test on **3D6**.
  - Target **same** type: test on **2D6**.
  - Target **larger** type: test on **1D6**.
  - *Example:* Lunar cruiser rams a cruiser on 2D6, an escort on 3D6, a battleship on 1D6.
- If the test is **failed**, the rammer misses and moves normally (still under All Ahead Full).
- **Resolving a ram** (damage from rams is **not deflected by shields**):
  - **Rammer** rolls **1D6 per point of its starting damage capacity** (not remaining). Each die **≥ target's armour** = 1 damage.
  - **Rammed ship** (hit in side or rear) rolls **D6 equal to half its starting damage**; each die **≥ rammer's front armour** = 1 damage.
  - **Head-on** (front to front), or if the rammed vessel is a **Defence**: the rammed vessel rolls D6 equal to its **full** starting damage. Defences always roll full starting damage as if prow-on.
- After resolving, the rammer continues its movement.

### Ramming summary (p. 56)
| Who | Dice rolled |
|---|---|
| Ramming ship | D6 = its starting damage |
| Rammed ship hit from side or rear | D6 = half its starting damage |
| Rammed ship hit from the front, or a Defence | D6 = its starting damage |

*Example:* Agrippa (Lunar, 8 hits) rams Unclean (Murder, Armour 5+) in the starboard side: Agrippa rolls 8D6 → 1,2,3,3,4,5,5,6 = 3 damage. Unclean rolls 4D6 vs Agrippa's front armour 6 → 3,3,5,6 = 1 damage.

- If either ship is destroyed and explodes from the ram, resolve the explosion immediately; both rammer and rammed are always in the explosion. If the rammed ship exploded, finish the rammer's move normally but it is slowed by the resulting Blast Markers (unless it has special rules).
- A rammer may later shoot or board; it may only board if it ends its **full** move in base contact with an enemy (not necessarily the rammed one).

## Disengaging From Combat (pp. 56–57)

- A vessel that **moves off the table edge** for any reason counts as **disengaged** (if not a hulk).
- Otherwise, to disengage a ship or escort squadron must pass a **Leadership test at the end of its Movement Phase**, with these modifiers:

| Circumstance | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Each Blast Marker within 5 cm | +1 |
| Any celestial phenomena within 15 cm | +3* |
| Each enemy ship or Ordnance marker within 15 cm | −1 |

\* For a tabletop feature such as an asteroid field or planet. If a tabletop **effect** (radiation burst, solar flare) is taking place, this bonus applies anywhere on the table.

- Add all modifiers together before rolling; rolls of 11 or 12 still automatically fail. (Ld9 + 3 BMs + asteroid field = Ld13, but must still roll ≤10.)
- **Passed**: remove the ship from the table; it may not return this game.
- **Failed**: the ship stays but **may not fire, launch ordnance, or use special orders (except Brace For Impact!)** this turn.
- Any ship/squadron reduced to **Leadership 1 or less** must attempt to disengage or move toward the closest table edge.
- **Escort squadrons**: if any single escort disengages, the whole squadron must immediately (and in subsequent turns) attempt to disengage. A disengaged escort squadron counts as **10% destroyed** (of the full squadron value), or **25%** if it was crippled (at least half its escorts destroyed, rounding down) before disengaging.
- **Capital ships in a squadron** may disengage separately, using their own base Leadership. If one fails, it drops out of the squadron and may not rejoin.
- **Victory points**: a ship that disengages gives the enemy **10% of its points value**, or **25% if crippled** before disengaging. (Not in addition to the 25% for remaining crippled on the table at game end.)

## Flying Bases Overlapping (p. 57)

- Ships may overlap (fly over/under each other) and stack. Use stemless bases or markers with a direction line when needed. Legal, but stacking drastically weakens shields (see Blast Markers).

## Stacking Ships and Escort Squadrons (p. 57)

- Cruisers and escorts may stack to follow battleships and other large-base models. Use contact markers if models don't physically fit.
- Ships should only stack/overlap as an unavoidable consequence of movement or to attain a particular shooting solution — not merely because it is allowed.
- A ship forced to stand still is targeted as **Defences**. Minimum move to avoid this: **5 cm**.
